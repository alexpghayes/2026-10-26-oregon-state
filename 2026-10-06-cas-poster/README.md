# Estimating spillovers in noisy networks via latent space models

Social networks are notoriously difficult to measure. Study participants report different friends depending on the precise language of surveys, they forget day-to-day social contacts, and reports from different participants often disagree. This noise in measurements of social networks poses a serious problem for most current methods of estimating social effects, which typically require that all social connections are precisely observed. We address this limitation by proposing a latent space model for social contagion, where influence operates via proximity in latent space rather than via explicit friendship ties. Our approach offers two significant advantages. First, we demonstrate that estimators derived under our latent space model can consistently estimate social contagion even under misspecification, when social influence genuinely does operate along network edges. Second, the latent space approach enables us to develop reliable estimators when social ties are measured with error. This is possible because latent structures remain estimable even when networks are observed with noise.
